When AI builds your site,
guidance matters more than generation.

Northstar is a concept for a guided AI website builder that treats personalisation as part of generation — not a repair step after it.

DesignerHarsh Kakroo
ScopeEnd-to-end product concept
PlatformDesktop-first, WordPress-hosted
AudienceSmall-business owners
The fastest AI builder won't win. The one that makes users feel the result is actually theirs will.
01 — Problem

Speed is solved. Specificity isn't.

AI builders generate plausible sites in seconds. What they haven't solved is why those sites still feel like they could belong to any business in the category.

01
Invisible understanding
Users can't tell what the AI understood before generation starts. Confidence gaps lead to distrust in the first output — and a higher restart rate.
02
Polished but interchangeable
Generated sites look coherent on first glance and forgettable on second. Service-specific trust signals, local credibility, and brand voice are missing or buried.
03
The edit cliff
The handoff from "AI made this" to "now you edit it" is abrupt. Users reach the builder not knowing what's safe to change — and momentum collapses.

02 — Users & Jobs

One flow, three different expectations.

The user spectrum isn't a simple skill axis — it's a range of what trust means. Beginners need confidence. Operators need efficiency. Power users need control they can reach quickly.

Primary
First-Time Founder
Launching a local service business or side hustle
  • Help me describe my business without knowing website terminology
  • Show me what you understood before building anything
  • Make sure my site doesn't look like every other landscaper in the city
  • Tell me what to change first so I don't break what's working
Primary
Practical Operator
Running a small business with limited time and high standards
  • I need this to feel credible, not just functional
  • My trust signals are specific — they need to actually show up
  • Refinement should take minutes, not sessions
  • The CTA has to match how my customers actually contact me
Secondary
WordPress-Savvy Builder
Has edited WordPress themes before — skeptical of shallow AI output
  • I'll tolerate the guided flow if I can reach real editing quickly
  • I want to see why one version differs from another — not just that it does
  • Don't hide editor access behind two more onboarding steps
  • Keep AI context visible when I start editing so I can trace what changed
03 — AI Decision Logic

The AI is visible, traceable, and correctable.

Most AI builders treat the model as a black box that delivers an answer. Northstar treats every AI decision as a surface that can be explained, questioned, and adjusted — without technical language.

Design principles for surfacing AI decisions
Principle 01

Show understanding before showing output

The brief confirmation step exists so users can see and correct what the AI understood before committing to a generation pass. Trust is built before output, not negotiated after it.

Principle 02

Explain why, not just what

Every AI recommendation includes a rationale tied to the business brief. "This leads with your trust signals because your goal is quote conversion" is useful. "This uses a warm palette" is not.

Principle 03

Surface uncertainty honestly

When the AI lacks information to generate something specific, it flags the gap rather than filling it silently with convincing-looking placeholder content.

Principle 04

Make every AI step reversible

Users can correct the brief, switch recommendations, undo refinements, or override at any point. The system invites scrutiny — because scrutiny builds trust.

Refinement belongs inside generation — not after it. Every step shapes intent before output appears, so users compare meaningfully and enter editing with momentum.

Three-layer control model

The same flow serves beginners and power users by surfacing control progressively — never asking for more than the user needs to give at each step.

🗣

Layer 1 — Plain-language brief

Business type, tone, goals, trust signals. Structured inputs, not open prompts. No terminology required.

🎛

Layer 2 — Guided refinement

Outcome-framed suggestions, one-tap controls. Business improvements, not tool jargon. Accessible from preview.

⚙️

Layer 3 — Advanced edit handoff

Full editor with preserved AI context. First edit recommended. Expert escape hatch that never breaks the flow.

06 — Edge Cases

Where the flow has to stay honest.

Each failure mode has a defined response that keeps the user moving without hiding what went wrong.

Edge Case 01
Output feels too generic
The generated homepage is structurally sound but could belong to any landscaping business in the category. Trust signals are vague; copy doesn't reference the service area.
Response: Highlight generic sections in the preview. Offer focused refinement actions tied to the specific gap — "Make this feel more Portland" or "Add specific service types."
Edge Case 02
Brand details are incomplete
User has a business name and services but no images, uneven tone, or missing proof points like reviews or certifications.
Response: Flag missing inputs before generation. Continue with a workable draft and show clearly which gaps are lowering specificity — not blocking progress.
Edge Case 03
User wants more control early
The user wants to change layout structure or section order before guided refinement finishes. The flow feels too slow for their skill level.
Response: "Edit directly" is visible at every preview step. AI context is preserved in the editor so early access doesn't mean starting cold.
Edge Case 04
Versions look the same
The user can't identify meaningful differences between the recommended version and the alternate. Comparison doesn't feel like a real decision.
Response: Annotate structural differences in tone, emphasis, and layout. Never surface alternates that are cosmetically different but functionally identical.
Edge Case 05
AI misunderstood the business
The generated site over-indexes on the wrong service, audience, or tone — the copy sounds like a different kind of business.
Response: Let the user correct the brief from the visible summary — not from scratch. Show which brief inputs produced which outputs so corrections feel targeted.
Edge Case 06
Momentum drops at handoff
User reaches the editor with the site loaded but doesn't know what to change first. The guided experience ends and the blank editor begins.
Response: One highlighted first edit and a short list of unresolved items. Context panel stays visible but compressed — supportive, not intrusive.
07 — Key Decisions & Tradeoffs

Where the product judgment lives.

These are the choices that define the concept — each one accepted a real cost.

Decision 01
Desktop-first, no mobile flow
Reviewing site structure, comparing versions, and making editing decisions are meaningfully better on larger screens. The generation-to-edit journey depends on spatial layout comparison that collapses at mobile sizes.
Cost: excludes users who only have mobile access. A responsive companion flow would be required in a v2 scope.
Decision 02
Guided brief over open prompt
Blank prompts shift the difficulty onto the user. Structured inputs with a visible summary ensure the AI uses the right information — and the user can verify it before output appears.
Cost: experienced users may find structured input slower. The "edit directly" escape hatch addresses this without removing the guided path.
Decision 03
One recommendation, not equal alternates
Three options with no recommendation creates analysis paralysis. One recommendation with visible AI rationale builds confidence and respects the brief — users can still compare and override.
Cost: users who disagree may feel the product is opinionated. The comparison step exists specifically for this case.
Decision 04
Refinement is not a restart
Treating personalisation as a repair phase after generation creates the wrong mental model. Building refinement into the flow treats every change as a continuation of intent.
Cost: increases system complexity and requires the AI to maintain state across refinement steps — a real v1 engineering constraint.
Decision 05
Plain-language diffs in comparison
Design vocabulary creates distance for non-designer users. Business-language differences — tone, emphasis, CTA framing — make the comparison actionable for the actual audience.
Cost: some nuance in visual differences is lost. Visual comparison is what the preview is for — comparison handles the decision logic.
Decision 06
Publish readiness is advisory, not a gate
Blocking publish behind a completeness check undermines user agency. Surfacing prioritised flags gives users the information to decide — not a correction loop.
Cost: users may publish with placeholder contact details if they dismiss the warning. This is a user decision the product respects.
08 — What I'd Validate Next

The questions that would define the real roadmap.

These are the assumptions built into the concept that user research would need to confirm — or challenge.

1
Does version comparison increase confidence — or introduce doubt?
The concept assumes showing two distinct versions with plain-language diffs helps users feel deliberate about their choice. The risk is it surfaces uncertainty about a recommendation they were already comfortable with. Test: compare task completion and confidence ratings between a single-recommendation flow and a two-version comparison.
2
Which refinement controls produce visible, credible changes?
Outcome-framed controls are designed to be more useful than style-specific controls. Some may produce changes too subtle for users to notice or trust. Test: measure whether users can describe what changed — without prompting — after applying each control.
3
Where do experienced WordPress users want to enter — and do they stay?
The concept assumes savvy users will tolerate the brief and style steps if "edit directly" is visible throughout. The risk is they skip the entire guided phase. Test: observe where power users reach for the escape hatch and whether they regret skipping earlier steps.
4
How much AI explanation is useful before it becomes noise?
The AI reasoning panels are designed to build confidence. Users already satisfied with the output may read explanations as uncertainty signals. Test: A/B test explanation visibility and measure forward momentum vs hesitation at each decision point.
5
Does the one-edit recommendation reduce editing anxiety — or feel prescriptive?
Naming a single first edit is intended to reduce the overwhelming feeling of entering a full editor. The risk is users interpret it as a constraint. Test: qualitative interviews at the handoff moment to distinguish helpful guidance from felt restriction.
09 — Assumptions & Open Questions

What this concept is built on.

Assumptions made

  • Desktop is the right primary context for generation and editing review
  • A structured brief produces better AI output than an open prompt for this audience
  • Beginners need the AI to surface its reasoning before trusting its output
  • Experienced users will accept a guided brief if a direct edit path is always visible
  • Three style directions is enough variety without being overwhelming
  • Plain-language comparison rows outperform visual side-by-side for non-designers
  • Refinement that builds on the base output is safer than full re-generation
  • A single recommended first edit reduces anxiety more than a fully open editor
  • Publish readiness should be advisory, not a gate
  • Most users need confidence and momentum more than maximum control at the start

Open questions for validation

  • Does version comparison help or does it undermine confidence in the recommendation?
  • Which refinement controls produce visible, credible changes vs cosmetic tweaks?
  • At what point do experienced users want to break out — and do they come back?
  • How much explanation is helpful before it reads as uncertainty or delay?
  • Does a recommended first edit feel supportive or limiting in the editor?
  • Is three style directions the right number, or does it depend on business category?
  • How does the flow perform for non-service businesses?
  • What's the right level of specificity the AI can claim from a lightweight brief?
